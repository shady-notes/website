Notes that live just above your screen.

A notes app you keep open costs you a window. A notes app you have to launch costs you the thought. Shady Notes costs you a flick of the scroll wheel.

Scroll near the menu bar
The shade slides down over whatever you're doing. Scroll the other way and it's gone. Direction is configurable and auto-detects mouse or trackpad.
One global hotkey
⌃⌥⌘↓ (Control-Option-Command-Down), rebindable, toggles the shade from inside any app. No window switching, no Dock, no Cmd+Tab.
The amber pull tab
A small tab hangs below the menu bar while the app runs. Click to toggle. It shrinks to a quiet dot when your mouse leaves.
Pin, window, minimize
Pin the shade open, detach it into a regular window, or strip the chrome for a distraction-free surface.

No lock-in. Just markdown.

Every note is a standalone .md file in a folder you choose, by default ~/Documents/ShadyNotes/. Point Obsidian at the same vault. Edit a note in VS Code and watch it reload in the shade. Grep it. Back it up. It's your disk.

No server and no sync service hold your notes, and you don't need an account to write. Only metadata is scanned at launch and a note loads when you open it, so the shade scales to vaults with thousands of notes. 

$ ls ~/Documents/ShadyNotes/
projects/    journal/     plugins/
ideas.md     groceries.md meetings.md

$ cat ideas.md
# Ideas

- [x] a notes app with no window
- [ ] that opens with a scroll
- [ ] and never holds my files hostage

Markdown that renders as you write.

A WYSIWYG editor over real markdown source. Never a proprietary format with an export bolted on.

Live markdown
Markers hide as you type and reveal when the cursor enters the line. Checkboxes, real tables, callouts, wikilinks and slash commands. ⌃E shows the raw source.
Tabs, stars and search
Tabs that persist across restarts, starred notes, vault-wide search and a VS Code-style find bar.
Plugins in the box
Daily Notes, Kanban and Tasks. Build your own as a single .md file with a YAML front-matter DSL, like this one.

Before you download.

Does it run on my Mac?
One download runs natively on Apple silicon and Intel Macs, on macOS 13 or later.
Will macOS let me open it?
Yes. It's signed and notarised by Apple, so there's no "unidentified developer" warning. The first time you open it, macOS asks whether you're sure you want to open an app downloaded from the internet; that's the normal first-run prompt. Click Open.
Do I need an account?
Not to write. The trial starts when the app does. You only sign in if you subscribe, so the app can unlock the paid features.
What happens after the 14 days?
The app drops to Free. Your notes, the editor and the shade keep working exactly as before; the extra themes, plugins and revision history lock until you subscribe.
